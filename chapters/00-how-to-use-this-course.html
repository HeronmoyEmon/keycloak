0. How to Use This Course

This chapter has no IAM content in it — it's the owner's manual. Read it once, set up two tools, and you're ready for chapter 1. Everything here is about how you'll study, not what you'll study.

Why this matters

A course you don't know how to navigate gets abandoned around chapter 6, not because the material was too hard but because the reader never understood what "done" looks like for a chapter, where the score is kept, or when they're supposed to actually open a terminal. Five minutes here saves that. This chapter also sets expectations correctly: if you came here to learn "which buttons to click in the Keycloak admin console," this course will frustrate you until roughly chapter 20 — and that delay is deliberate, not a stall.

What "mastery" means in this course

"Mastery" here has a specific, narrow test: could you explain why Keycloak — or any IAM product — is built the way it is, not just where its buttons are? Admin-console tutorials teach the buttons. This course teaches the concept each button implements, and only then shows you the button.

Concretely, that means the curriculum is split into two kinds of chapters that always travel in pairs conceptually, even though Keycloak itself doesn't show up until Part IV:

  • Concept chapters (Parts I–III) teach an idea — what a directory service is, what OAuth's authorization code flow does, what "least privilege" means — with no product in sight. They include short "In Keycloak" callouts that point forward at the exact admin-console screen that will implement the idea, and which later chapter covers it in depth.
  • Keycloak chapters (Parts IV onward) open by naming which concept chapter they are an implementation of, then get hands-on with a real, running Keycloak.
Key idea

The failure mode this course is built to prevent: finishing all the Keycloak chapters, being able to configure a realm from memory, and still being unable to answer "why does Keycloak force a redirect to its own login page instead of letting the app collect the password?" If you can explain the why, not just drive the console, the concept chapters did their job.

How grading and quizzes work

There's no account, no server, and no instructor. Every chapter ends with a short quiz, drawn from a larger per-chapter question pool, and rendered by the same QuizEngine component you'll see at the bottom of this page. A few mechanics worth knowing up front:

  • Each attempt pulls a random subset of questions from that chapter's pool and shuffles both the question order and each question's answer options — so retaking a quiz isn't just memorizing positions.
  • Your score for a chapter is stored in your browser's localStorage under a key like iam:quiz:how-to-use-this-course — it records your best score, your most recent score, and how many times you've attempted it.
  • Nothing is sent anywhere. Clearing your browser's site data (or opening the course in a private window, or on a different device) resets your recorded progress for that quiz, because there is nowhere else it's stored.
  • There's no pass/fail gate — a low score doesn't lock later chapters. The quiz is a self-check, not a bouncer. Use it honestly: if you're guessing on more than one or two questions, that's a signal to re-read the section above it, not to retake until you get lucky.

Each chapter also has a self-check section before the quiz — open-ended questions with no shown answer. Those are for you to answer out loud or in notes before you even reach the graded quiz; if you can't answer one in a sentence or two, that's the part of the chapter to re-read.

Environment setup

Parts I–III (chapters 1–18) are concept-only. You don't need Keycloak, Docker, or anything else running to work through them — read, think, do the self-checks, take the quiz. The first hands-on lab against a real Keycloak instance is chapter 20, at the start of Part IV. Chapters 13–15 (the OAuth/OIDC protocol chapters) have curl-only exercises that don't need Keycloak either, just a terminal.

That said, it's worth installing the tools now so they're not a blocker later. You'll want:

Try it

Open a terminal right now and run docker --version and java -version. You're not installing or configuring anything yet — just confirming both commands exist and print a version string, so you're not discovering a missing tool in the middle of chapter 20's lab. If either command isn't found, install Docker from its official docs and a JDK distribution (e.g. Adoptium/Temurin) before you reach Part IV — there's no rush to do it today.

Pitfall

Don't install Keycloak itself yet. A handful of readers jump ahead and spin up a Keycloak container before chapter 1, then spend Parts I–III clicking around an admin console with no conceptual map for what any of it means. The concept chapters are short on purpose — resist the urge to skip to "the real thing" until chapter 20 tells you to.

The study loop

Every chapter (lab chapters and concept-only chapters alike) follows the same loop. Knowing it up front means you'll never wonder "wait, am I done with this chapter?"

Three organizations you'll keep meeting

Starting in chapter 2, every worked example, scenario, and quiz question across all 50 chapters draws on the same three fictional organizations instead of a new made-up company every time: Northwind Apps, Riverside Health, and Meridian Trust. Chapter 2 introduces who each one is and what kind of identities each manages — this chapter is just the heads-up that they're coming, so when they show up you recognize them as recurring characters, not one-off trivia.

In Keycloak

Each of the three organizations eventually gets its own realm in a shared local Keycloak instance — the admin console's realm switcher (top-left dropdown) is literally how you'll move between "being" Northwind, Riverside, or Meridian during labs. Realms as a concept are covered in chapter 4; configuring one for real is chapter 21.

Glossary terms introduced here

This chapter is navigational rather than conceptual, so it introduces no new glossary terms of its own — the master glossary starts filling in from chapter 1 onward.

Takeaways

  • "Mastery" in this course means being able to explain why Keycloak is built the way it is, not just where its buttons are.
  • Concept chapters (Parts I–III) and Keycloak chapters (Parts IV+) are two halves of the same pair — skipping the concept half is the exact failure mode this course exists to prevent.
  • Quizzes are self-graded, localStorage-only, and ungated — a low score never blocks the next chapter, but it is a signal to re-read.
  • Self-check questions have no shown answer on purpose — they're a thinking prompt, not a quiz.
  • Parts I–III need no Keycloak instance at all; the first real hands-on lab is chapter 20.
  • Install Docker and a JDK now, but don't install Keycloak itself before chapter 20 — there's nothing for it to do yet, and doing so tends to produce button-clicking without context.
  • The curriculum has 11 Parts: orientation, three concept Parts, six Keycloak-hands-on Parts, and two reference/capstone Parts.
  • Three recurring fictional organizations — a SaaS company, a hospital network, and a bank — will anchor every worked example starting chapter 2; chapter 0 is only the preview.
